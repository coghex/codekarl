# Codekarl vision: Diplomacy, war and peace

Part of [the vision](../vision.md), moved verbatim from it on 2026-10-21. Owner decisions are marked **Firm** with their date; everything else is **Open** or a **Proposal**, as there.

## Diplomacy, war and peace (owner, 2026-10-18 17:31Z–20:10Z)

Answers to ck-guide's three questions of 17:25Z (at 17:31Z), its three of 17:31Z (at 17:37Z), its three of 17:38Z (at 17:58Z), its three of
17:58Z (at 18:12Z), its two of 18:12Z (at 18:12:56Z), its two of 18:13Z (at 18:18Z), its two of 18:18Z (at 18:22Z), its two of 18:22Z (at
18:29Z), its three of 18:29Z (at 18:34Z), its two of 18:34Z (at 18:39Z), the owner's own addition at 18:53Z, its three of 18:53Z (at
19:01Z), its two of 19:02Z (at 19:05Z), its two of 19:05Z (at 19:14Z), its two of 19:57Z (at 20:06Z), and its two of 20:06Z (at 20:10Z).
References to EU4 describe the owner's intent, not a claim about EU4's exact rules.

- **Firm. A war requires a casus belli.** Mission trees should hand them out, and war with natural enemies and rivals should be allowed.
  - **Open:** how natural enemies and rivals are defined, and how they give a valid casus belli.
- **Firm. A complicated peace-terms system "like eu4".** Different actions have a war-score value. A deal is proposed, bilaterally, and
  its acceptance is based on how the opponent values those terms.
  - **Firm (owner, 17:37Z). Bilateral peace "is a must".** Proposing concessions in victory should still cost war score; it simply
    increases the loser's acceptance of the deal.
- **Firm. War-score values are scaled by the type of war,** "like in eu4". *Owner's examples:* a holy war gives a discount to conquering
  territory, whereas in a simple skirmish between rivals, taking territory has a huge war-score penalty. So it benefits the player to go to
  war with the best casus belli available for what they want.
- **Firm. A winning defender gets a boost,** since taking land after winning a defensive war should be easier.
- **Firm. Cores.** Countries should see lost territory as theirs by right, and should take it back very cheaply.
  - **Firm (owner, 17:37Z). Cores come from two places,** "novel, not like eu4":
    - **Culture.** *Owner's example:* France should consider all land occupied by at least 50% French cultures to be cores.
    - **Mission trees** can also grant cores.
    - "Those are the two situations i forsee."
  - **Firm (owner, 17:37Z). Other cores are temporary.** Any other core a country has is temporary: if the population requirement drops
    below 50%, a timer begins, and after 100 years the core disappears.
    - **Firm (owner, 18:12Z: "yes").** If the qualifying majority returns, the countdown is cancelled; a later drop starts a fresh 100
      years. Permanent mission cores are unaffected.
  - **Firm (owner, 17:58Z). Cultures belong to groups** (called **subgroups** since 19:05Z, below). The culture is a big part of what
    defines the borders naturally: this system "will naturally keep countries like france big, countries like switzerland small, and help
    countries like german minors unite and eventually form germany".
    - *The owner's proposed French culture group:* breton, gallo, norman, picard, parisian, angevin, saintongeais, gascon, languedocian,
      provincial, savoyard, limosin, bourbonnais and lyonaisse; "there are probably others i want".
    - *Further owner examples:* basque is its own group; alsacien is in the North German group; romand is in the Swiss group; catalan is in
      the Catalan group.
    - These are the owner's game taxonomy, recorded with the owner's spelling; they need research and are not a verified historical
      classification.
  - **Firm (owner, 17:58Z). Mission-granted cores expire** if the required cultural majority is gone, unless they are permanent cores.
  - **Firm (owner, 17:58Z). Permanent cores,** "like eu4": mission trees should be able to hand out, in certain specific situations,
    permanent cores that never expire.
    - *Owner's example:* when England is formed, it should get permanent cores on the entirety of Greater Britain, including Ireland. These
      let England slowly conquer the Highlands and Ireland over hundreds of years, even though the Celtic and Norse-Gael groups stay
      separate.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:12Z). Cores can be shared.** Normandy and France would both see Norman territory as their core. Each location has a
    list of tags that needs to be regularly checked and updated.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:12Z). No accepted minorities.** The owner does not want any sense of accepting minorities, which EU4 and EU5 do.
    Whether a minority is accepted is instead based on the cultural opinion of the host nation's culture towards the guest culture (see
    "Cultural and religious relations" under Populations). It should directly affect satisfaction. There is no granting cultures rights,
    "at least not yet". (This answered ck-guide's 17:58Z question about accepted cultures, where ck-guide had suggested keeping cultural
    acceptance and territorial entitlement separate; the owner rejected an acceptance designation altogether.)
    - **Corrected (owner, 18:18Z).** The owner had said that this opinion would also directly affect the coring rules, then corrected it:
      "this is me being wrong, i mispoke, coring rules will not be effected". Cultural opinion does not change core eligibility.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:12:56Z: "no").** Favourable cultural opinion does not let a culture outside the group count towards the 50%
    threshold (ck-guide's example: Basque inhabitants counting towards a French core).
  - **Firm (owner, 18:12:56Z). A tag has one defined culture,** and "perhaps this can be changed"; how is undecided.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:18Z). Pops can change culture.** Each culture has two values: **cultural tradition**, the defensive stat, and
    **cultural influence**, the offensive stat.
    - *Owner's examples (proposed game content, needing research):* Parisian should have strong values of both, forcing nearby cultures to
      begin slowly converting. A culture like Albanian or Armenian would have low influence but high tradition: hard to convert. Something
      like Gothic in Circassia would have low values for both: a culture being quickly converted and on its way out.
    - How the values are defined is undecided: "perhaps influence comes from works of art, and tradition comes from isolation. but there
      could be a myriad of factors".
    - **Firm (owner, 19:14Z). Tradition and influence each have a baseline value, with a decaying value on top of it.** How fast it decays,
      and whether the same applies to proselytism and fervor, is not stated.
    - **Firm (owner, 19:14Z). Cultural values and traits.** ck-guide's 19:05Z candidates (commerce, military service, religious devotion,
      craftsmanship and attachment to local traditions, alongside literacy) "all sound good, it could be anything". Many of these traits
      would be culture-specific, or perhaps specific to the supergroup or subgroup. The owner will implement them "when i get around to that
      part of the project"; no list or effects are chosen now.
      - **Firm. Traits can change.** *Owner's examples (possibilities):* if a culture goes through a traumatic event, perhaps it gets a
        trait that boosts its cultural tradition baseline; a culture with a negative trait could perhaps lose it through some mission-tree
        event. "There could be all sorts of stuff like this."
    - *ck-guide's 18:18Z note (not an owner decision):* assimilation could still change cores indirectly, by changing a population's
      cultural make-up under the population-share rules above.
    - **Firm (owner, 18:22Z). Trade is the main driver** of cultural spread. (It is the main route, not necessarily the only one.)
    - **Firm (owner, 18:22Z: "i think we could"). A possible ruling-culture boost,** "but there is always the threat of a subject culture
      that is different than the main culture getting out of hand".
    - **Firm (owner, 18:29Z). Influence comes from both crown support and ordinary activity:** "yes, both, all of those, funding art,
      schools, and just building an economy" (answering ck-guide's 18:22Z question).
    - **Refined (owner, 19:01Z).** *Earlier (18:53Z):* a pop changes culture only if it has the same religion. Now:
      - **Firm. Religions belong to religious groups.**
      - **Firm. Religion sets the conversion speed.** The exact same religion converts culture the fastest; the same religious group at half
        speed; different religious groups not at all. *Owner's example:* if France conquers a Lutheran country whose culture is alsacien, it
        converts at half speed.
    - **Firm (owner, 18:53Z: "im thinking"). Supercultures.** Cultures should only convert within the same supergroup, a larger group
      above culture groups. The owner sees a problem in EU4 and EU5, where France could conquer North Africa and after a few hundred years
      the population is 90% French, "that is incorrect". At the same time, alsacien should slowly convert to lorrain even though they are in
      different groups. African lands should stay African.
      - **Replaced (owner, 19:01Z).** *Earlier (18:53Z):* membership need not be mutually exclusive, so France could convert conquered
        Italian lands to provincial. Now **each culture belongs to exactly one superculture**, "so north french and south french wont really
        bleed into eachother".
      - **Firm (owner, 19:01Z).** Conversion can still happen at half speed when the superculture differs but the "subgroup" is the same
        (confirmed at 19:05Z: introducing supergroups means the existing culture groups, such as French, North German and Swiss, are now
        called **subgroups**). *Owner's examples:* "at a border like limousin/bourbonaise, there would be less
        conversion, but there would still be some, but at a border like breton/gallo, the bretons should slowly eat away at gallo". Which
        superculture each of these cultures belongs to is not stated.
      - Whether cultures of different supercultures and different culture groups can ever convert is not restated; the restriction to the
        same superculture otherwise stands.
      - **Firm (owner, 19:05Z: "yes"). The half-speed penalties multiply.** Crossing both a religious boundary within one religious group
        and a superculture boundary within one subgroup gives quarter speed (½ × ½), as ck-guide suggested at 19:02Z.
      - The owner chose "superculture" rather than "race", which "could add all sorts of problematic connotations".
      - *The owner's proposed starting list:* north european, south european, north african, sub saharan african, south african, north
        american, south american, eastern european, middle eastern, south asian, steppe, east asian and polynesian ("i think that should
        cover all of them"). This is provisional game content, with the owner's spelling.
      - **Firm (owner, 19:01Z).** Oceania "is too big and unimportant to get that granular with it"; "australasian could be fine" as the
        name (answering ck-guide's 18:53Z remark that Polynesian would not cover all of Oceania). Whether it replaces or adds to polynesian
        is open.
    - **Firm (owner, 19:01Z: "yes"). The destination culture is not the tag's culture by default.** The owner agreed with ck-guide's 18:53Z
      reading that conversion should favour a compatible, influential culture nearby rather than always the tag's primary culture: "this is
      also opposite of eu4 and 5 but i think its better, i dont want to implant parisians in provence, although we could have a few,
      cultures should be relatively independent of nations". A bonus to the state culture is possible, but typically culture changes based
      on influence and tradition.
  - **Open:** how religious groups are defined; which cultures belong to which supercultures; what routes besides trade spread culture, and
    how trade exposure is measured; how large a ruling-culture boost would be; what happens when a subject culture gets out of hand; how
    much crown patronage and economic activity each contribute; how tradition and influence are set and how conversion is calculated; whose
    opinions set satisfaction (*ck-guide's 18:12Z suggestion, not an owner decision:* each pop's own culture's opinions); and how the
    population share is measured (by location or wider area).
- **Firm (owner, 18:29Z). Religion uses a similar system with different language.** Religions have **proselytism**, the offensive value,
  and **fervor**, the defensive value. Religions could have rules that change these values.
  - Unlike cultures, large numbers of pops could convert all at once (the owner's "i think").
  - Spread is more radius-based, around monasteries and churches or cathedrals (compared with trade as the main driver of cultural spread).
  - "Then we should have religious missions perhaps and forced conversion as a military option. there are all sorts of things we could
    do." These are possibilities, not decisions; the earlier missionary example is under "Other satisfaction factors" in
    Populations.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:34Z). Every religion has a leader.** For Catholicism that is the pope; if the pope is in charge of the Papal States,
    the player can choose the Papal States as their tag and change the religion that way. "We will need a whole new system to handle
    that", left open for now.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:34Z). A king can start a new religion in the same group,** such as Anglicanism, and is then in charge of its rules.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:34Z: "any of those things could be an example").** ck-guide's 18:29Z examples of what could trigger a large
    conversion are all possible: a successful missionary, a ruler adopting a new faith, a major religious event, or accumulated local
    pressure reaching a threshold. None is chosen as a rule.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:34Z). Forced conversion changes only the state religion,** which then cannot be changed until the treaty has
    expired. "We could also force convert a small percentage of the pops too", as a possibility.
  - **Firm (owner, 18:39Z). Religious buildings keep their clergy's religion when the state religion changes,** unless the crown owns the
    building. The state religion is "really an 'in name only' thing for the most part".
  - **Firm (owner, 18:39Z). Missionaries are funded almost exclusively privately, by the church.** (This answers ck-guide's 18:34Z question
    whether clergy fund religious buildings and missions themselves; the crown is not ruled out.)
  - **Firm (the owner's intended balance).** The church should be the wealthiest pop group in the game until at least the 1500s. This is a
    design target, not a verified historical claim.
  - **Open:** the religious-leadership system; how religion groups are defined, and the conditions and cost of founding a new religion;
    the mass-conversion mechanics; treaty duration and any forced-conversion share; what happens to a crown-owned religious building when
    the state religion changes; and how proselytism and fervor are set.
- **Firm. Revanchism.** A country that loses a lot of territory gets a revanchism value that further decreases the cost in future wars:
  "a country that has lost a lot of territory should be able to conquer a ton in their next war".
  - **Firm (owner, 17:37Z). The discount applies to any conquest,** not only to recovering former territory.
- **Firm (owner, 17:37Z). Occupation modifies demands.** There should be a huge penalty to demanding land in a place no soldiers of the
  demanding country have touched.
  - *Owner's example:* France will not have Scotland occupied, since landing troops on English soil would be very difficult with the English
    navy. So even if France has revanchism and beats the British, the AI would decide on taking English lands on the continent, such as
    Aquitaine and Normandy.
  - **Firm (owner, 17:58Z). It is current occupation** that counts, not occupation earlier in the war.
- **Firm (owner, 20:06Z). All interactions between countries are treaties.** Vassals, tributaries, marches and fiefdoms use the same treaty
  system as alliances and defensive pacts. Subjects remain separate tags, and players should be able to select vassals to play.
  - **Firm (owner, 20:10Z). Terms are negotiable, and they decide the relationship.** As much as possible should be negotiable, and the
    resulting treaty is then classified as one of those relationships. *Owner's examples:* a treaty with troops and no money would be a
    march; there could also be specific cultural or religious examples.
  - **Firm (owner, 20:10Z). A tag can refuse a treaty obligation, but there would be consequences.** What they are is not stated
    (ck-guide's 20:06Z question suggested, for example, giving the overlord a casus belli; that is not an owner decision).
- **Firm (owner, 20:06Z). Annexation.** In the "form England" mission example the owner expected full annexation, but is "not sure on the
  history exactly"; whichever it is, such outcomes are applied manually by each mission. Diplomatic interaction will have both forced and
  mutually agreed annexations.
- **Firm. No personal relationships between rulers;** diplomacy uses only countries' opinions of each other.
  - This is separate from characters' traits and skills (see "Characters").
- **Open:** which terms a peace deal can contain, beyond territory (ck-guide's 17:25Z examples were payments in particular coins or goods,
  transferring business ownership, releasing subjects and enforcing a law; none was chosen); the war-score values and scaling; how the AI
  values terms; which terms treaties can contain, and how a treaty is classified from them; the consequences of refusing an obligation; and
  what annexation does to assets, debts and laws.
