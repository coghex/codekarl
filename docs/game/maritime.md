# Codekarl vision: Ships and maritime trade

Part of [the vision](../vision.md), moved verbatim from it on 2026-10-21. Owner decisions are marked **Firm** with their date; everything else is **Open** or a **Proposal**, as there.

## Ships and maritime trade (owner, 2026-10-18 19:22Z–19:57Z)

Answers to ck-guide's two questions of 19:14Z (at 19:22Z), its three of 19:23Z (at 19:30Z), its two of 19:31Z (at 19:34Z), its two of 19:34Z
(at 19:42Z), its two of 19:42Z (at 19:44Z), and its two of 19:44Z (at 19:57Z). The owner feels this is an area where both EU4/EU5 and
Victoria 2/3 fall short of what the owner wants.

- **Firm. Anybody can own ships.** Ships are like moving buildings, with stores, a production method, employees and so on.
- **Firm. Shipyards produce ships** and store them in a way that only other port cities can trade them. Once an individual purchases a
  ship, it becomes a building at that shipyard's location: owned not as a wealth item but as an asset, like a building.
  - *Owner's example:* a merchant may have a shipping business simply by owning a lot of boats.
- **Firm (owner, 19:30Z). A purchased ship starts empty and hires a crew where it was built.** *Owner's example:* if a merchant bought a
  ship in Dublin, the building would start empty and slowly hire Irish pops until it can sail home. It would then still have an Irish crew,
  which could cause some pop satisfaction drop, so merchants should prefer to hire locally among their own culture subgroup.
- **Firm (owner, 19:30Z). Privately owned ships trade when not busy.** The merchant decides on the trade they want to make, then dispatches
  the ship. It leaves with goods to sell, or coin, buys what the merchant wants at the trading destination, and returns to the trading
  source with the new goods, selling them, hopefully for a profit. (The owner wrote "selling them, hopefully for a profit at the
  destination", after describing the return to the source.)
  - If prices are different when the ship arrives, that could be a loss for the merchant: "that just how it goes".
  - Enough bad trades and a merchant may be forced to put their ship up for sale; if the ship is old enough, perhaps scuttling would make
    more sense.
- **Firm (owner, 19:30Z: "yea i think that is right"). A ship's production method is how it is equipped and used,** as ck-guide put it at
  19:23Z (for example cargo transport, troop transport, fishing, armed trading or privateering, with changes needing supplies and time).
  The owner calls these missions: different missions take different ratios and numbers of pops, and supplies.
  - *Owner's example:* a military mission would fill up with cannon shot and medical supplies; a trade mission still needs those things,
    but less. (Which supplies exist in which era is not settled.)
  - The stockpiles deplete during the mission, so merchants need to estimate whether they can complete it before taking it.
  - **Firm. All ships can take all missions for now;** ship type matters less, but different ships might be more or less suited to a task.
- **Desired, if simple to implement (owner, 19:34Z: "yes, that sounds like something i would like if its simple to implement").** As
  ck-guide put it at 19:31Z: a ship can buy provisions and ammunition at ports along the way, with the merchant budgeting for those stops,
  and if supplies run short unexpectedly it seeks a suitable port automatically, even if that means abandoning the planned trade.
- **Firm (owner, 19:34Z). Naval combat calculates which ships are destroyed and which are captured.** If there is no crew for a captured
  ship, it needs to be scuttled.
  - **Firm (owner, 19:42Z). A captured ship is kept only if it is valuable and there is enough extra crew.** The owner said yes to
    ck-guide's 19:34Z idea of redistributing the victor's surviving crew, "unless the captured ship is below the threshold for value": some
    ships will be junk, and those should be scuttled.
  - **Firm (owner, 19:42Z). Cargo may be moved off before scuttling, depending on the situation.** *Owner's examples:* a Spanish galleon
    full of gold, yes; a cargo ship full of fertiliser, no. "We will need logic here."
- **Firm. The crown builds and maintains its own vessels** using the same ownership system.
- **Firm. In wartime, private vessels can be drafted, requisitioned or hired.**
- **Proposed by the owner (19:22Z: "we could also").** A privateering system: merchants fly a black flag and fight enemy ships on the high
  seas.
  - **Firm (owner, 19:44Z). Only permitted targets for now;** independent piracy is left for later. Who grants permission, and how, is not
    stated (ck-guide's 19:42Z question suggested the crown authorising attacks on specified enemy tags).
- **Firm (owner, 19:44Z: "ideally yes"). Captured sailors use the existing prisoner system,** with ransom, release and hiring (see
  "Battles, garrisons and control").
- **Firm (owner, 19:57Z). Blockades are part of the naval siege system.** All ships can blockade any port, blocking all trade until they
  are removed forcefully. A naval blockade on a port gives bonuses to any offensive armies besieging the city or fort.
  - The player needs an indication that they are being blockaded, because this will cause problems for the local economy.
  - "It would be nice" to let the player allow only exports or only imports, or perhaps whitelist and blacklist certain tags.
- **Firm (owner, 19:57Z). Missions can use several ships.** A merchant should be able to send any number of ships on a mission, and all
  ships on the mission can use their holds for goods.
  - **Firm. Merchants can rent ships,** from each other or by request from the crown, and rented ships come with their crew. *Owner's
    example:* a merchant could rent some carracks or galleys to protect more vulnerable cargo ships.
  - Lost rented ships become debts the merchant has to handle, unless they are insured.
  - These features are for the far future: "im not to worried about the details".
- "There are lots of details to work out here, we can do that or leave it open."
- **Open:** whether ships can carry other people's cargo for a fee (ck-guide asked at 19:23Z; not answered); the value threshold for keeping
  a ship, and the logic for choosing which cargo to save; how much crew a ship needs to sail versus to fight; who permits privateering
  targets, and how; how much time and which supplies a change of mission takes; which trade a blockade stops and how selective blockades
  work; rental terms, debts and insurance; how ships move; what drafting, requisitioning and hiring mean for the owner, crew and
  compensation, and how ships are returned; and what captured ships and cargo become.
